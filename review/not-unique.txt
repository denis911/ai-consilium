Yes, this concept is highly relevant and aligns with one of the most prominent architectural patterns in modern AI engineering. The [AI Consilium](https://github.com/topics/ai-consilium) repository you shared represents a production-hardened execution of a concept often referred to in academia and industry as "LLM Ensembling," "LLM Councils," or "Multi-Agent Debate/Consensus". [1, 2, 3] 
The idea of using a "jury" or "council" of distinct models to arrive at a single, deterministic answer exists across several hobby projects, research papers, and developer frameworks:
## 1. Academic & Research Agents

* Multi-Agent Debate Frameworks (MAD): Multiple papers published between 2023 and 2026 (such as the Multi-Agent Debate for LLM Judges paper) explore the mathematics behind having LLMs iteratively critique each other. Research proves that forcing diverse models to debate or vote heavily reduces individual model biases and hallucinations. [2, 4, 5, 6] 
* Mixture-of-Agents (MoA): Popularized by Together AI, this is a layered research architecture where multiple independent LLMs generate separate drafts, and a high-level aggregator model synthesizes them into a single superior response. [7] 

## 2. Similar Open-Source & Hobby Projects

* LLM Council / PolyCouncil: Several repositories on GitHub share a nearly identical philosophy to ai-consilium. For instance, frameworks like LLM Council run local or API-driven LLMs concurrently, run a cross-critique phase, and produce a structured, aggregated output. PolyCouncil focuses on orchestrating and voting across local models running on LM Studio. [3, 8] 
* Consilium (Hugging Face Hackathon Project): A multi-LLM debate platform built during a recent Gradio Agents hackathon. It utilizes the Model Context Protocol (MCP) to let major models argue complex questions and settle on a response using majority voting or ranked-choice rules. [9] 
* niftymonkey/ai-consensus: A web-app designed around multi-model collaboration via OpenRouter. It allows users to watch multiple frontier models stream text simultaneously and use a designated model to evaluate the "consensus". [10] 

## 3. Real Commercial Software / Developer Tools

* Nous Research (Hermes Agent Infrastructure): Major development groups are explicitly building voting and consensus protocols into their core multi-agent workflows. For example, issues inside advanced frameworks discuss building native ConsensusEngine classes to handle majority, supermajority, and unanimous voting strategies among sub-agents. [7, 11] 
* Enterprise AI Gateways: Many corporate systems route sensitive user prompts to two separate models (e.g., GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet) simultaneously. If their responses diverge significantly in vector space, the system flags the prompt for human review before proceeding, preventing automated errors in banking and compliance. [2] 

## Why the ai-consilium project stands out
While the core idea of multi-LLM consensus isn't entirely unique, this specific implementation is incredibly well-engineered for a few reasons:

   1. Z-Score Outlier Detection: Instead of relying on a "vibe check" or basic LLM synthesis, it uses mathematical distance (384-dimensional embeddings) and statistical Z-scores to isolate which model is hallucinating or deviating from the norm.
   2. The "Knowledge Flywheel": The way it hooks back into DuckDB for local hybrid search (Dense Vector + Full-Text Search) over your existing Obsidian notes makes it a self-improving brain. It doesn't just write to Obsidian; it reads from it to ground its next consensus session.
